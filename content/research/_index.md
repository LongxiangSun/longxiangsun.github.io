# Research

I am interested in crustal deformation, active faults, and earthquake processes, especially how faults accumulate and release strain over the earthquake cycle. My research focuses on interseismic deformation and fault coupling, with the goal of understanding how faults move, lock, creep, and eventually contribute to earthquake generation.

I mainly work with Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) and Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) observations, together with geophysical modeling. In simple terms, I use satellites, ground-based observations, and a fair amount of coding to turn tiny movements of the Earth's surface into something we can hopefully understand.

My research has mainly focused on the **San Andreas Fault system** in California and the **Xianshuihe Fault** on the eastern margin of the Tibetan Plateau. I am particularly interested in fault slip rates, interseismic coupling, strain accumulation, and their implications for earthquake hazard.

My research on the San Andreas Fault system focuses on the spatially heterogeneous deformation and interseismic coupling of major active faults in California. By combining GNSS and InSAR observations with three-dimensional fault modeling, I investigate fault slip rates, creep, locking, and the distribution of slip deficit along the fault system.

### Xianshuihe Fault

The Xianshuihe Fault is one of the major active strike-slip fault systems on the eastern margin of the Tibetan Plateau. I use multi-source GNSS and InSAR observations to investigate its interseismic deformation, long-term slip rates, strain accumulation, and fault coupling. A particular focus is placed on the spatial heterogeneity of fault locking and its relationship with recent earthquake ruptures.

## Methods and Data

My work combines:

* **GNSS** for measuring three-dimensional crustal motion
* **InSAR** for mapping spatially continuous surface deformation
* **Three-dimensional elastic block-fault modeling** for fault kinematics and interseismic coupling
* **Geophysical and numerical modeling** for interpreting crustal deformation and fault behavior
* **MATLAB, GMT, and other computational tools** for data processing, inversion, and visualization

Ultimately, I hope to better understand how deformation is distributed across active fault systems and how interseismic strain accumulation may be related to earthquake generation and seismic hazard.
